# Impeccable — portfolio ("The Gaming Table")

Design context for this project. Loaded by the `impeccable` skill and its companions (`craft`, `shape`, `polish`, `critique`, `typeset`, `layout`, `colorize`, etc.) so design work stays anchored to who this is for and how it should feel.

## Design Context

### Users

**Primary persona — a hiring manager or prospective client evaluating Matt Bayne.** They arrive from a résumé link, a LinkedIn profile, or a referral, usually on a laptop, often with several other candidate tabs open. They are time-pressed and lightly skeptical: they have seen a hundred generic portfolios and are scanning for a reason to keep reading.

**Context when using it.** A first visit is short and exploratory — skim the hero, judge the craft in the first few seconds, decide whether the work is worth a deeper look. A motivated visitor then opens one or two artifacts (a deck, a viz, a write-up) to assess depth. Some arrive on mobile from a phone link and must get the same impression.

**Job to be done.** Quickly judge whether Matt is worth interviewing or hiring — by reading evidence of real product/engineering work, not adjectives. The portfolio's job is to make that evidence feel premium, credible, and memorable, and to lower the friction from "intrigued" to "contact him."

**Stakeholder audience.** This is a public, external-facing artifact (live at mattbayne.dev). It represents Matt's personal brand and taste, so the bar is "would a senior design-literate reviewer respect this," not "does it function."

### Brand Personality

**Three words: crafted, premium, playful-with-restraint.**

- **Crafted** — every surface should read as deliberately made by someone who sweats detail: gold gradient borders, layered shadows, hand-tuned textures. Nothing looks like a default template.
- **Premium** — the tabletop-game metaphor is executed at a luxury tier (think a collector's-edition board game, not a kids' game). Materials feel rich: dark walnut, parchment, burnished gold.
- **Playful-with-restraint** — the RPG character-sheet / quest-board conceit is delightful and distinctive, but it never tips into gimmick. The metaphor serves the content (real work as "quests"); it does not bury it.

**Emotional goals for first 30 seconds:**

1. **Intrigue** — "I haven't seen a portfolio like this." The character sheet and skill cards signal taste and confidence immediately.
2. **Credibility** — "this person can actually build and ship." The craft of the site itself is the first proof point; the work behind each quest is the second.
3. **Ease** — "I can find the good stuff fast." The metaphor is legible at a glance; scannable headlines (`subject`) lead, themed kickers (`title`) are secondary, and contacting Matt is one obvious gold button away.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Visual tone — premium tabletop / collector's-edition RPG.** Rich, tactile, editorial. The reference feel is a beautifully produced board game or an illuminated character sheet, rendered as software — not a flat SaaS marketing page and not a literal fantasy-kitsch theme.

**Theme — warm dark/parchment, not a generic light or dark mode.** Dark walnut background (`#1A1410`) with parchment surfaces (`#F2EAD9`) and burnished gold (`#C8973E`) as the lead accent. Skill identity colors (Belief `#8B2E3A`, Communication `#2E5A8B`, Woo `#2E6B4A`, Ideation `#5E3A8B`, Maximizer `#B8860B`) are used for the five Clifton Strengths, sparingly elsewhere.

**Typography.** Cormorant Garamond for display, Inter for body/UI, Crimson Pro for flavor/serif copy, JetBrains Mono for stats/pips, Cinzel for the monogram seal only. Type carries the "crafted" feeling — generous display contrast, restrained body.

**Textures are CSS/SVG, never raster.** Wood grain (`repeating-linear-gradient`), parchment (linen weave), gold borders (the 2px padding-trick gradient on a `bg-surface` inner). Keep them subtle; they are atmosphere, not noise.

**Anti-references (what this must NOT become):**

- **Fantasy-kitsch.** No dragons, no faux-medieval blackletter headers, no parchment-scroll clip art, no torch-flicker effects. The theme is *premium collector's game*, executed with taste — not a renaissance-faire stall.
- **Generic SaaS / AI-slop portfolio.** No purple-to-blue gradient hero, no glassmorphism cards on a dark gradient, no "Hi, I'm Matt 👋" with a waving emoji, no identical card grid of skills with neon accents, no gradient text.
- **Theme over substance.** The game metaphor must never make the actual work harder to read. If a decision makes a quest's real subject less scannable to win on theme, it is wrong.

### Design Principles

1. **Craft is the first proof point.** The portfolio argues Matt's competence by *being* impeccably made. Alignment, spacing, and micro-detail must survive a senior reviewer's scrutiny — `/polish` and `/audit` standards apply, not "good enough."

2. **Metaphor serves content.** "Quest," "character sheet," and "skill card" are framing devices for real artifacts. The real headline (`subject`) leads; the themed kicker (`title`) is a small gold eyebrow above it — never swap them back.

3. **Premium means restraint, not maximalism.** Richness comes from materials and detail (gold edges, layered shadows, warm textures), not from piling on effects. When in doubt, remove — `/distill` and `/quieter` over `/bolder` for this brand.

4. **One reading rhythm.** Shell (persistent-nav) artifact pages are calm, readable, editorial — the "illuminated manuscript" grammar: gold section kickers, drop-cap opener, left-set prose, gold-framed `ProductShot` evidence. Every artifact follows it; don't invent per-page layouts. (Immersive full-screen mode was deleted as unused — it may return only with real deck content.)

5. **Accessibility is WCAG 2.2 AA, held without exceptions.** 4.5:1 text contrast (muted-on-parchment stays at `#6B5F4F` ≈5.2:1 — do not lighten it back), visible focus rings, full keyboard nav, and respect for `prefers-reduced-motion`. The premium feel must not cost legibility.
